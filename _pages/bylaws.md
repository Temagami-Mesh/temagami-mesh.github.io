# Temagami Mesh Networking

# By-law No. 1

*Organizational By-law of the Association*  
*and, upon incorporation, of the Corporation*

A dual-status governing instrument prepared so that the same text governs the unincorporated association and becomes the organizational by-law of an Ontario not-for-profit corporation under the *Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010* (ONCA).

**Adopted:** September ???, 2026

*Effective immediately as the constitution of the Association and, on the Certificate Date, as By-law No. 1 of the Corporation.*

## Article 1 — Interpretation and Dual Status

### 1.01 Definitions

In this By-law, unless the context otherwise requires:

**“Act”** means, after the Certificate Date, the *Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010* (Ontario) and the regulations under it, as amended. Before the Certificate Date, “Act” means the ONCA used as an interpretive guide so that Association practice will match corporate practice.

**“Association”** means the unincorporated not-for-profit association known as Temagami Mesh Networking, constituted by this By-law.

**“Board”** means the board of directors of the Organization.

**“Certificate Date”** means the date on the certificate of incorporation issued to the Corporation under the Act.

**“Chair”** means the chair of the Board.

**“Corporation”** means the Ontario corporation without share capital to be incorporated under the Act under the proposed name Temagami Mesh Networking Inc., or such other ONCA-compliant name as the first directors file.

**“Director”** means an individual occupying the position of director of the Organization.

**“Member”** means a member of the Organization.

**“Officer”** means an officer of the Organization.

**“Organization”** means the Association before the Certificate Date and the Corporation on and after the Certificate Date. Rights, obligations, offices, records, resolutions and property of the Association continue as those of the Corporation without further act, subject to Article 17.

**“Ordinary Resolution”** means a resolution that is submitted to a meeting of Members and passed by a majority of the votes cast, or a resolution signed by all Members entitled to vote.

**“Principles”** means the ten community principles set out in section 2.03 and reproduced in Schedule D, as they may be refined under section 2.04.

**“Purposes”** means the purposes set out in section 2.01 and, after the Certificate Date, the purposes set out in the articles.

**“Region”** means the Temagami region and neighbouring lakes, canoe routes and wilderness areas that are unserved or underserved by commercial telecommunications networks.

**“Special Resolution”** means a resolution that is submitted to a meeting of Members and passed by at least two-thirds of the votes cast, or a resolution signed by all Members entitled to vote.

### 1.02 Interpretation

1. Words importing the singular include the plural and vice versa. Words importing one gender include all genders. Headings are for convenience only.
2. Terms defined in the Act have the same meaning in this By-law.
3. If any provision of this By-law is inconsistent with the articles or the Act, the articles or the Act prevail.
4. If any provision is invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions continue in effect.
5. A reference to a person includes an individual, corporation, partnership, trust, unincorporated organization, government or agency.

### 1.03 Dual status — governing principle

1. Before the Certificate Date this By-law is the constitution of the Association and a contract among the Members.
2. On the Certificate Date this By-law becomes the organizational by-law of the Corporation, without the need to re-enact its text, subject only to any change required so that it does not conflict with the articles or the Act.
3. The Association is not a partnership, joint venture or business corporation. No Member has an ownership or equity interest in the Organization or in the mesh infrastructure stewarded by it.

## Article 2 — Purposes

### 2.01 Purposes

The Organization is constituted, and the Corporation shall be incorporated, exclusively for purposes other than profit. The purposes of the Organization are the establishment and operation of a community communications organization for the purposes of:

- **(a)** promoting, developing and supporting resilient, low-cost, off-grid mesh radio communications, including text messaging and position sharing, for residents, visitors and travellers in the Region;
- **(b)** coordinating the deployment, operation and stewardship of community mesh infrastructure and open technical practices so that the network remains freely accessible, community-stewarded and not operated as a subscription or for-profit telecommunications service;
- **(c)** advancing public safety, emergency preparedness and civic improvement by enabling backup communications during storms, fires, medical events, power outages and other disruptions of conventional networks;
- **(d)** educating the public and providing technical information, forums and assistance concerning open-source mesh networking and its community use; and
- **(e)** other complementary purposes not inconsistent with these purposes.

### 2.02 How the Organization is organized and operated

1. The Organization shall be organized and operated exclusively for the Purposes and for no other purpose, including any purpose of profit.
2. Commercial activity, if any, is permitted only to advance or support one or more of the Purposes.
3. No part of the Organization’s income, profits or property, or of any accretion to the value of its property, may be distributed, directly or indirectly, to a Member, Director or Officer except in furtherance of the Purposes or as otherwise permitted by the Act (including reasonable compensation for services and reimbursement of reasonable expenses).
4. Membership does not confer any right to a share of surplus, any residual claim on assets, or any right to require the network to be operated as a paid service.

### 2.03 Community and network principles

The Organization adopts the following ten principles as operational rules for its activities, decisions and community practice. They are intended to reduce friction, encourage experimentation and keep the project sustainable. The Organization is both a technical project and a community of practice — part mesh network, part amateur-radio tradition, part neighbourhood resilience initiative. Directors, Officers and Members shall have regard to these principles when acting for the Organization.

1. **Open source and transparency.** Work created by or for the Organization — including hardware designs, documentation, maps, integration code, bots and guides — shall be released openly under a permissive licence appropriate to the work (including MIT, GPL or CC-BY-SA) unless a funder or a third-party owner lawfully requires otherwise. Contributors shall be credited. Parallel complementary projects are welcome; coordination on core local compatibility is encouraged. The Organization shall contribute back to upstream open-source projects, including Meshtastic and related firmware and tools, where it reasonably can.
2. **Experimentation and building.** Members are encouraged to tinker, prototype and innovate, including custom antennas, do-it-yourself nodes, sensor integrations, LoRa experiments and amateur-radio cross-band setups. Personal experiments and personal nodes are autonomous except where they would impair core infrastructure or violate this By-law. Sharing builds, failures and discoveries is part of the Purposes.
3. **Amateur-radio spirit.** Even where activity uses licence-exempt ISM or LoRa bands, the Organization shall operate in the spirit of the amateur-radio tradition: considerate, loyal, progressive, friendly, balanced and community-oriented. That includes technical excellence, patient mentoring of newcomers, and operating practices that improve the experience of everyone sharing the spectrum and the mesh.
4. **Local benefit and resilience.** The primary purpose of the community mesh is to serve people who live, cottage, work and visit in the Region. Emergency preparedness, communications during outages and storms, and practical uses that make island and remote life safer take priority over novelty for its own sake.
5. **Inclusive and accessible.** Participation is open at every skill level, from a beginner who wants a simple node to an experienced amateur or tinkerer. Gatekeeping and condescension have no place in the Organization’s work. The Organization shall maintain beginner-friendly onboarding and clear public documentation.
6. **Share knowledge and document.** Knowledge developed in the course of the Organization’s work should be written down and shared. The Organization shall maintain a public knowledge base so that documentation, install photos, range-test results, configuration examples and troubleshooting guides do not remain siloed in private chats.
7. **Legal, responsible and considerate operation.** The Organization and persons acting for it shall comply with applicable Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada requirements and other law. They shall use appropriate power levels, avoid harmful interference, and remain mindful of other users of the band. Emergency or priority traffic on the mesh may be given precedence. Good node practice is part of membership in good standing.
8. **Privacy, property and environment.** No node or antenna shall be placed on private property, a shared dock or a structure without the informed permission of the person who has the right to grant it. Deployments shall respect user privacy and be thoughtful about what data is broadcast on the mesh. Deployments shall respect the lake’s natural aesthetics, remain unobtrusive, and leave no trace.
9. **Network health and thoughtful growth.** Adding nodes or changing settings affects everyone. Major router placements, channel plans and firmware versions used on core infrastructure shall be coordinated so that the mesh remains reliable, redundant and performant. Quality and coverage are favoured over sheer quantity of nodes. Growth is welcome when it remains manageable.
10. **Respectful disagreement; keep it enjoyable.** Participants shall assume good intent. Technical choices, node placement and project direction shall be discussed openly and on the evidence, with regard to the long-term health of the project. Personal attacks, harassment and abusive behaviour are grounds for discipline under section 4.08. The project is voluntary.

Infrastructure deployed or accepted into the community mesh is stewarded for community use. Title may be held by the Organization, by a Member who has placed a node under a written stewardship arrangement, or as the Board otherwise records. Stewardship arrangements shall preserve free community access consistent with the Purposes. There is no single owner of the mesh in the sense of a right to exclude ordinary community use. The Organization shall not operate the community mesh as a subscription telecommunications service and shall not require payment as a condition of ordinary access.

Nothing in this section prevents the Organization from accepting donations, grants or in-kind contributions, from paying for goods and services, from recovering out-of-pocket costs of optional equipment sold at or near cost, or from the Board adopting acceptable-use, safety, spectrum, siting and interconnection practices consistent with these principles.

### 2.04 Living document and decision-making practice

1. Schedule D sets out the public text of the Principles as adopted with this By-law. The Board may publish and update a community edition of the Principles (including on the project website) with commentary, examples and onboarding notes. An update to the community edition does not amend this By-law.
2. The Board shall not, by community-edition update alone, repeal or materially weaken any of the ten numbered principles in section 2.03. A change of that kind is a by-law amendment under Article 14.
3. If the community edition conflicts with this By-law, the articles or the Act, this By-law, the articles or the Act prevail.
4. For core infrastructure — shared routers, primary channels and public map curation — the Board and any technical committee shall aim for rough consensus among active contributors before a material change. Personal nodes and smaller experiments remain autonomous if they do not impair core infrastructure or violate this By-law.
5. New Members shall be pointed to the Principles early so that expectations are clear.

## Article 3 — Office and Records

### 3.01 Head office / registered office

The head office of the Association, and the registered office of the Corporation, shall be in Ontario at the address fixed by the Board from time to time. Until the Board otherwise resolves, that address is the address of the Secretary. A post-office box is not a registered office under the Act.

### 3.02 Records

The Organization shall keep, at the office or at another place in Ontario approved by the Board: this By-law and any other by-laws; a register of Members, Directors and Officers; minutes of meetings and written resolutions of Members and of the Board; accounting records; consents to act as Director; and, after the Certificate Date, a copy of the articles and any unanimous member agreement. A Member may examine the records that the Act (or, before the Certificate Date, this By-law) entitles a member to examine, during ordinary business hours on reasonable notice.

### 3.03 Seal

The Organization may have a seal in a form approved by the Board. The absence of a seal does not affect the validity of any document.

## Article 4 — Members

### 4.01 One class

The Organization has one class of Members. Each Member is entitled to receive notice of, attend and vote at meetings of Members, and has one vote.

### 4.02 Conditions of membership

Membership is open to any individual who:

- **(a)** is at least 18 years of age;
- **(b)** supports the Purposes and agrees to abide by the Principles;
- **(c)** applies in the form required by the Board; and
- **(d)** is admitted by resolution of the Board.

A corporation or other organization may be admitted as a Member only if the Board first adopts a written policy allowing organizational members. An organizational Member exercises its vote through one individual it designates in writing.

### 4.03 Founding Members

The individuals who adopt this By-law on the date first written above are the Founding Members and are Members without further application. The first Board named in section 6.03 are Members so long as they hold office, unless they sooner resign membership.

### 4.04 No transfer; no equity

A membership is not transferable. A membership is not an ownership interest, share, unit or security.

### 4.05 Dues

The Board may fix membership dues, including a dues of nil. Failure to pay dues within 60 days after written notice is a ground for termination under section 4.08.

### 4.06 Term and resignation

Membership continues until it terminates under this By-law. A Member may resign by written notice to the Secretary. Resignation does not relieve the Member from any obligation incurred while a Member.

### 4.07 Automatic termination

Membership terminates automatically on death, resignation, dissolution of an organizational Member, liquidation or dissolution of the Organization, or expiry of a term if the Board has admitted the Member for a fixed term.

### 4.08 Discipline or termination for cause

1. The Board may, by resolution, discipline a Member or terminate a membership for violating the articles, this By-law (including the Principles in section 2.03), a written policy of the Board, or for conduct that, in the Board’s reasonable opinion, is materially injurious to the Purposes, to the community mesh, or to the safety or dignity of participants. Harassment, personal attacks and abusive behaviour are cause.
2. The Board shall give the Member at least 15 days’ written notice of the proposed action and the reasons for it. The Member may deliver a written submission not less than five days before the end of that period. The Board shall consider that submission before making a final decision.
3. The Board shall act in good faith and in a fair and reasonable manner. A Member whose membership is terminated ceases to be a Member on the effective date stated in the Board’s decision.

## Article 5 — Meetings of Members

### 5.01 Annual meeting

1. The Association shall hold an annual meeting of Members at least once in every calendar year.
2. After the Certificate Date, the annual meeting shall be held not later than 18 months after the Certificate Date and subsequently not later than 15 months after the last annual meeting and not later than six months after the end of the preceding financial year, on a day and at a place in Ontario fixed by the Board, or entirely by telephonic or electronic means.
3. The business of the annual meeting includes: receipt of the agenda and of minutes; consideration of the financial statements; the report of the auditor or review engagement person, if any; appointment or waiver of an auditor or review engagement person for the coming year; election of Directors; and any special business set out in the notice.

### 5.02 Special meetings

The Board may call a special meeting of Members at any time. The Board shall call a special meeting on the written requisition of Members holding at least 10 percent of the votes that may be cast at a meeting of Members. If the Board does not call the meeting within 21 days after receiving the requisition, any Member who signed the requisition may call it.

### 5.03 Notice

1. Notice of the time and place of a meeting of Members shall be given to each Member entitled to vote, to each Director and to the auditor or review engagement person, if any, not less than 10 days and not more than 50 days before the meeting.
2. Notice may be given in accordance with Article 13. If the meeting may be attended by telephonic or electronic means, the notice shall include instructions for attending, participating and voting by those means.
3. Notice of a meeting at which special business will be transacted shall state the nature of that business in sufficient detail to permit a Member to form a reasoned judgment on it, and shall state the text of any Special Resolution to be submitted.
4. A Member may waive notice. Attendance at a meeting is a waiver of notice unless the Member attends for the express purpose of objecting to the transaction of business on the ground that the meeting is not lawfully called.

### 5.04 Financial information before the annual meeting

On request, a Member shall be provided, not less than five business days before the annual meeting, with a copy of the approved financial statements and of any auditor’s or review engagement report.

### 5.05 Chair of Members’ meetings

The Chair shall preside. If the Chair is absent, the Directors present shall choose one of their number to preside. If no Director is present or willing, the Members present shall choose one of their number to preside.

### 5.06 Quorum

Quorum for a meeting of Members is the greater of two voting Members and 25 percent of the voting Members, except that if there are more than 40 voting Members, quorum is 10 voting Members. A person who attends by telephonic or electronic means is present for quorum. If a quorum is present at the opening, the Members may proceed even if a quorum is not present throughout.

### 5.07 Voting

1. Each Member has one vote. Questions are decided by Ordinary Resolution unless the Act, the articles or this By-law requires a Special Resolution.
2. Voting is by show of hands or the electronic equivalent unless a ballot is demanded by a Member entitled to vote. The chair of the meeting does not have a second or casting vote.
3. A proxy is permitted. A proxy shall be in writing, signed by the Member, and deposited with the Secretary before the meeting begins or at such earlier time as the notice specifies, not exceeding 48 hours (excluding Saturdays and holidays) before the meeting.
4. A resolution in writing signed by all Members entitled to vote on that resolution is as valid as if it had been passed at a meeting.

### 5.08 Electronic meetings and voting

A meeting of Members may be held entirely by one or more telephonic or electronic means, or by any combination of in-person attendance and telephonic or electronic means, if all persons entitled to attend are able reasonably to participate. A person who attends or votes by those means is present at the meeting. A vote may be conducted entirely by telephonic or electronic means, or by a combination of those means and voting in person.

### 5.09 Adjournment

The chair of the meeting may, with the consent of the meeting, adjourn it. If a meeting is adjourned by one or more adjournments for an aggregate of less than 30 days, no notice of the continued meeting is required if the time and place, and any electronic-attendance instructions, are announced at the time of adjournment.

## Article 6 — Directors

### 6.01 Number

The Board consists of a minimum of three and a maximum of nine Directors. Until the Members by Special Resolution fix a different number within that range, the number of Directors is three. After the Certificate Date the articles shall state the same minimum and maximum. The number of Directors to be elected at an annual meeting is the number then in office, unless the Members or, if empowered by Special Resolution, the Directors determine a different number within the range.

### 6.02 Qualifications

A Director shall:

- **(a)** be an individual at least 18 years of age;
- **(b)** not have the status of bankrupt;
- **(c)** not have been found incapable of managing property or incapable by a court in Canada or elsewhere;
- **(d)** be a Member, or become a Member within 10 days after election or appointment; and
- **(e)** consent in writing to serve, before or within 10 days after election or appointment.

### 6.03 First Directors

The first Directors of the Association, who shall serve until the close of the first annual meeting of Members or until their successors are elected or appointed, are:

1. Bond Keevil (founding Director)
2. Darcy Fortin (founding Director)
3. ________________________________ (founding Director)

On incorporation, the first Directors named in the articles shall be those individuals, unless the Founding Members have meanwhile elected a different Board. Each first Director of the Corporation must sign the consent required by the Act.

### 6.04 Election and term

1. Directors are elected by Ordinary Resolution of the Members at the first meeting of Members and at each annual meeting.
2. The term of a Director is from the meeting at which the Director is elected or appointed until the close of the next annual meeting, or until a successor is elected or appointed.
3. A Director may be re-elected. There is no term limit unless the Members by Ordinary Resolution adopt one.
4. Election may be by acclamation if the number of candidates does not exceed the number of offices to be filled.

### 6.05 Ceasing to hold office

The office of a Director is vacated immediately if the Director:

- **(a)** resigns by written notice to the Organization, effective when received or at the later time specified in the notice;
- **(b)** dies or becomes bankrupt;
- **(c)** is found to be incapable by a court or incapable of managing property under Ontario law;
- **(d)** ceases to be qualified under section 6.02 and is not restored to qualification within 10 days; or
- **(e)** is removed by Ordinary Resolution of the Members before the end of the Director’s term.

### 6.06 Filling vacancies

1. If the Members remove a Director, the Members may fill the vacancy by Ordinary Resolution.
2. If there is not a quorum of Directors, or if there has been a failure to elect the minimum number of Directors, the Directors then in office shall without delay call a special meeting of Members to fill the vacancy. If they fail to call that meeting, or if there are no Directors in office, any Member may call it.
3. A quorum of Directors may fill any other vacancy among the Directors.
4. A Director elected or appointed to fill a vacancy holds office for the remainder of the unexpired term.

### 6.07 Remuneration of Directors

1. Directors serve as Directors without remuneration and shall not directly or indirectly receive any profit from occupying the office of Director.
2. A Director may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses incurred in the performance of Directors’ duties.
3. A Director may be paid reasonable remuneration for services provided to the Organization in a capacity other than as Director — including planning, deploying, hosting, maintaining or training in respect of mesh nodes — if:
   - **(a)** the amount is considered reasonable by the Board;
   - **(b)** the Board approves the payment by resolution passed before the payment is made;
   - **(c)** the Director complies with Article 11; and
   - **(d)** if the Organization is or becomes a charitable corporation, the Act and the law applicable to charitable corporations are also complied with.
4. The Board may, by resolution, adopt a published schedule of deployment and service honoraria (for example, a fixed amount per successful node deployment). A published schedule does not excuse the disclosure and recusal required by Article 11 when a Director is the proposed payee.

### 6.08 Standard of care

Every Director and Officer shall, in exercising their powers and discharging their duties, act honestly and in good faith with a view to the best interests of the Organization, and exercise the care, diligence and skill that a reasonably prudent person would exercise in comparable circumstances.

## Article 7 — Meetings of the Board

### 7.01 Calling meetings

Meetings of the Board may be called by the Chair or by any two Directors. For the first meeting of first Directors, any first Director may call the meeting on not less than five days’ notice.

### 7.02 Regular meetings

The Board may fix the time and place of regular meetings. A copy of that resolution sent to each Director is sufficient notice of those meetings.

### 7.03 Notice

Notice of a Board meeting shall be given to every Director not less than 48 hours before the meeting, or not less than seven days before the meeting if given only by mail. Notice is not necessary if all Directors are present and none objects, or if those absent have waived notice or consented to the meeting being held. A newly elected Board may, if a quorum is present, hold its first meeting immediately after the annual meeting without notice. A notice need not specify a place if the meeting is to be held entirely by telephonic or electronic means. If Directors may attend by those means, the notice shall include instructions for attending, participating and, if applicable, voting.

### 7.04 Quorum

A majority of the Directors then in office constitutes a quorum.

### 7.05 Chair and voting

The Chair shall preside. In the Chair’s absence the Directors present shall choose one of their number to preside. Each Director has one vote. Questions are decided by a majority of votes. The chair of the meeting does not have a second or casting vote. A resolution in writing signed by all Directors entitled to vote on it is as valid as if passed at a meeting.

### 7.06 Electronic meetings

A meeting of Directors may be held entirely by telephonic or electronic means, or by any combination of in-person attendance and those means, if all persons attending are able to communicate with each other simultaneously and instantaneously. A person who attends by those means is present at the meeting.

## Article 8 — Officers

### 8.01 Appointment

The Board shall appoint from among the Directors a Chair, and shall appoint a Secretary and a Treasurer, who may but need not be Directors. The offices of Secretary and Treasurer may be held by the same person (Secretary-Treasurer). The offices of Chair and president, if a president is appointed, may be held by the same person. The Board may appoint other Officers. An Officer holds office at the pleasure of the Board, and in any event until the earlier of the appointment of a successor, resignation, or death.

### 8.02 Duties of the Chair

The Chair presides at Board and Members’ meetings, provides leadership to the Board, and performs the other duties the Board assigns.

### 8.03 Duties of the Secretary

The Secretary gives or causes to be given notices, keeps minutes of Board and Members’ meetings, keeps the registers and minute book, certifies documents when required, and performs the other duties the Board assigns.

### 8.04 Duties of the Treasurer

The Treasurer keeps or causes to be kept the accounting records, works with the Board on budgets and financial statements, has charge of the Organization’s funds subject to Board direction, and performs the other duties the Board assigns.

### 8.05 Delegation

An Officer is responsible for the duties of the office and may delegate performance of any of those duties, but remains accountable to the Board.

## Article 9 — Committees

### 9.01 Committees of the Board

The Board may appoint a managing Director or a committee of Directors and may delegate any powers of the Directors except the powers the Act does not permit to be delegated. The Board may also establish other committees, fix their composition and terms of reference, and dissolve them at any time. A committee that includes persons who are not Directors is advisory only.

### 9.02 Technical and stewardship committee

Without limiting section 9.01, the Board may establish a technical and stewardship committee to advise on node siting, spectrum and radio practice, safety, interconnection, channel plans, firmware used on core infrastructure, public map curation, documentation of open practices, and relations with landowners, Indigenous communities, municipalities and other agencies. The committee shall have regard to section 2.04(4) (rough consensus on core infrastructure; autonomy of personal nodes). That committee does not itself hold title to Organization property and does not bind the Organization except as the Board authorizes.

## Article 10 — Financial Matters

### 10.01 Financial year

The financial year ends on December 31 in each year, or on such other date as the Board by resolution determines.

### 10.02 Banking and execution of documents

1. The Board shall designate the financial institution in which funds and securities are placed.
2. Deeds, transfers, assignments, contracts and other instruments may be signed by any two Officers or Directors. The Board may direct the manner in which, and the person by whom, a particular document or class of documents shall be signed.
3. Any Director or Officer may certify a copy of any instrument, resolution, by-law or other document of the Organization to be a true copy.

### 10.03 Borrowing

The Board may, for the Organization, borrow money, issue evidence of indebtedness, give security, and give guarantees, on the terms the Board considers appropriate. The Members may by Special Resolution restrict those powers, but a restriction expires at the next annual meeting.

### 10.04 Compensation for services; deployment honoraria

1. The Board may employ or retain persons, including Members and Directors (subject to section 6.07 and Article 11), to plan, deploy, host, maintain or train in respect of mesh infrastructure, and to carry on the administration of the Organization.
2. Compensation shall be reasonable in light of the work, the Organization’s resources, and amounts paid for comparable volunteer-supported technical work.
3. The Board may adopt a deployment honorarium schedule. Until the Board otherwise resolves, the Founding Members’ proposal of an honorarium of up to $300 per successful node deployment, payable only from funds then available for that purpose, may be implemented by Board resolution.
4. No compensation arrangement authorizes a distribution of surplus or a residual claim on assets.

### 10.05 Auditor or review engagement

The Members shall at each annual meeting appoint an auditor or a person to conduct a review engagement, or, if the Act permits, waive that appointment by extraordinary resolution (80 percent of the votes cast, or such other threshold as the Act then requires). Waiver, if permitted, expires at the next annual meeting.

### 10.06 No private benefit

Except as this By-law and the Act permit, the Organization shall not make its income available for the personal benefit of any Member, Director or Officer.

## Article 11 — Conflict of Interest

### 11.01 Disclosure

A Director who is a party to a material contract or transaction, or proposed material contract or transaction, with the Organization, or who is a director or officer of, or has a material interest in, any person who is a party to such a contract or transaction, shall disclose the interest in writing to the Organization or request that it be entered in the minutes. The Director shall not attend any part of a meeting during which the contract or transaction is discussed, and shall not vote on any resolution to approve it, except as the Act permits.

### 11.02 Honoraria and related-party work

A proposed payment to a Director or Officer for node deployment, hosting, maintenance or other services is a material transaction for the purpose of this Article. The interested Director may present factual information if asked, then shall leave the meeting while the Board deliberates and votes.

### 11.03 Charitable overlay

If the Organization is or becomes a charitable corporation, no Director shall, directly or through an associate, receive a financial benefit from the Organization unless the Act and the law applicable to charitable corporations are complied with.

## Article 12 — Protection of Directors and Officers

### 12.01 Limitation of liability

No Director, Officer or committee member is liable for the acts, neglects or defaults of any other Director, Officer, committee member or employee, or for any loss, damage or expense to the Organization through insufficiency or deficiency of title to property acquired for the Organization, or the insufficiency or deficiency of any security in which money is invested, or any loss arising from the bankruptcy, insolvency or tortious act of any person with whom money or effects are lodged, or any other loss which may happen in the execution of the office, provided that the person has complied with the Act and the articles and By-laws and has exercised their powers and discharged their duties in accordance with the Act. This section does not relieve a person from any liability the Act or other law does not permit to be limited.

### 12.02 Indemnity

The Organization shall indemnify each present and former Director and Officer, and each other person the Act permits the Organization to indemnify, to the maximum extent the Act permits, against all costs, charges and expenses, including an amount paid to settle an action or satisfy a judgment, reasonably incurred in respect of any civil, criminal, administrative, investigative or other proceeding in which the person is involved because of that association with the Organization, if the person acted honestly and in good faith with a view to the best interests of the Organization and, in the case of a criminal or administrative proceeding that is enforced by a monetary penalty, the person had reasonable grounds for believing that the conduct was lawful.

### 12.03 Insurance

The Board may purchase and maintain directors’ and officers’ liability insurance, and other insurance, in amounts the Board considers appropriate. Incorporation does not itself replace insurance. Until the Certificate Date, Members should understand that an unincorporated association does not confer limited liability.

## Article 13 — Notices

### 13.01 Method

A notice or other document required to be sent under this By-law may be sent by personal delivery, mail, courier, or electronic means to the address in the registers of the Organization. A notice sent by mail is deemed received on the fifth day after mailing. A notice sent by electronic means is deemed received on the day it is sent, if sent before 5:00 p.m. Eastern time on a day other than a Saturday or holiday, and on the next day that is not a Saturday or holiday otherwise.

### 13.02 Error or omission

The accidental omission to give notice to any person, or the non-receipt of a notice, does not invalidate any action taken at the meeting or pursuant to the notice.

## Article 14 — Amendment

### 14.01 By-laws

1. Subject to the Act and the articles, the Board may make, amend or repeal any by-law that regulates the activities or affairs of the Organization, except in respect of a matter that the Act requires to be confirmed by Special Resolution of the Members.
2. The Board shall submit the by-law, amendment or repeal to the Members at the next meeting of Members. The Members may confirm, reject or amend it by Ordinary Resolution. If rejected, it ceases to have effect. If amended, it takes effect as amended. If the Members do not confirm it, it ceases to have effect after that meeting.
3. A by-law change that the Act requires to be approved by Special Resolution of the Members (including a change to the conditions of membership, to membership transfer, to the manner of giving notice to Members, or to the method of voting by Members not in attendance) is not effective until that Special Resolution is passed.
4. Before the Certificate Date, sections 14.01(1) to (3) apply with necessary changes, and a change may also be made by Special Resolution of the Members without a prior Board resolution.

## Article 15 — Dispute Resolution

### 15.01 Good-faith discussion

A dispute among Members, Directors or Officers relating to the By-laws, the Principles or the affairs of the Organization shall first be raised in the ordinary community channels or in writing with the Board. Participants shall assume good intent and focus on evidence and the long-term health of the project. The Board shall attempt to resolve a written dispute within 30 days.

### 15.02 Mediation

If the dispute remains unresolved, any party may require mediation before a mediator agreed by the parties or, failing agreement within 14 days, designated by the Chair of the Board (or, if the Chair is a party, by the Secretary). Project maintainers may be asked to mediate informal technical disputes before this section is invoked. Formal mediation shall take place in Ontario, in person or by electronic means. Each party bears its own costs and they share the mediator’s fee equally, unless they agree otherwise.

### 15.03 Seat of disputes

The affairs of the Organization are governed by the laws of Ontario and the laws of Canada applicable in Ontario. Courts of Ontario have jurisdiction, without prejudice to any right under the Act.

## Article 16 — Dissolution

### 16.01 Decision to dissolve

The Association may be dissolved by Special Resolution of the Members. The Corporation may be dissolved in accordance with the Act.

### 16.02 Distribution of remaining property

After payment of all debts, obligations and liabilities:

- **(a)** no remaining property shall be paid to or distributed among the Members, Directors or Officers;
- **(b)** remaining property shall be distributed to one or more organizations that are organized and operated exclusively for purposes similar to the Purposes, that prohibit distribution of income to members, and that carry on activities in Ontario, as determined by the Board and confirmed by Ordinary Resolution of the Members; and
- **(c)** if at the time of dissolution the Organization is a public benefit corporation under the Act, remaining property shall be distributed only to a public benefit corporation with similar purposes or to a government or government agency, as the Act requires.

The articles of the Corporation shall contain a dissolution provision to the same effect.

### 16.03 Mesh infrastructure on dissolution

In applying section 16.02 the Board shall, so far as practicable, transfer stewardship of deployed community nodes to the successor organization or to landowners and operators who agree in writing to keep those nodes freely accessible for community use consistent with the Purposes.

## Article 17 — Incorporation and Transition

# Schedule B — Purposes

The purposes of the corporation are:

The establishment and operation of a community communications organization for the purposes of:

- **(a)** promoting, developing and supporting resilient, low-cost, off-grid mesh radio communications, including text messaging and position sharing, for residents, visitors and travellers in the Temagami region and neighbouring lakes, canoe routes and wilderness areas that are unserved or underserved by commercial telecommunications networks;
- **(b)** coordinating the deployment, operation and stewardship of community mesh infrastructure and open technical practices so that the network remains freely accessible, community-stewarded and not operated as a subscription or for-profit telecommunications service;
- **(c)** advancing public safety, emergency preparedness and civic improvement by enabling backup communications during storms, fires, medical events, power outages and other disruptions of conventional networks;
- **(d)** educating the public and providing technical information, forums and assistance concerning open-source mesh networking and its community use; and
- **(e)** other complementary purposes not inconsistent with these purposes.

**Special provisions** (in addition to the non-profit clause that Ontario inserts automatically):

1. The corporation shall have a minimum of three and a maximum of nine directors.
2. There shall be one class of members. Each member is entitled to one vote at a meeting of members.
3. A membership is not transferable.
4. No part of the corporation’s profits or of its property may be distributed to a member, director or officer except in furtherance of its activities or as otherwise permitted by the *Not-for-Profit Corporations Act, 2010*.
5. Upon the liquidation, dissolution or winding-up of the corporation, after satisfaction of all debts, obligations and liabilities, remaining property shall not be distributed among the members. It shall be distributed to one or more organizations that are organized and operated exclusively for similar purposes and that prohibit distribution of income to their members or, if the corporation is then a public benefit corporation, to a public benefit corporation with similar purposes or to a government or government agency, as required by the Act.

# Schedule D — Community Principles & Code of Conduct

*Public text adopted with this By-law, drawn from the Temagami Mesh Project principles page (last updated August 2026).*

As the Temagami Regional Mesh grows, the community is to remain welcoming, collaborative, and focused on building a reliable, open and useful off-grid communication network for the Temagami region. These ten principles guide activities, decisions and interactions. They draw from open-source practice, the spirit of the Amateur Radio Code, and the practical needs of a remote Northern Ontario community.

## 1. Embrace Open Source & Transparency

Everything we create — hardware designs, documentation, maps, integration code, weather bots, or hardware guides — is released openly. We use permissive licenses (MIT, GPL, CC-BY-SA) as appropriate and give clear credit to contributors. Parallel complimentary projects are welcome; coordination on core local compatibility is encouraged.

## 2. Actively Support Experimentation & Building

We explicitly encourage members who want to tinker, prototype, and innovate. Custom antennas, DIY meshtastic nodes, sensor integrations, LoRa experiments, or amateur radio cross-band setups are all valued. Share your builds, failures, and discoveries — this is how the network and our individual skills advance.

## 3. Uphold the Amateur Radio Spirit

Even though much of our activity uses licence-free ISM/LoRa bands, we operate in the spirit of the classic Amateur Radio Code: Considerate • Loyal • Progressive • Friendly • Balanced • Community-oriented. We strive for technical excellence, patient mentoring of newcomers, and operating practices that improve the experience for everyone sharing the spectrum and the mesh.

## 4. Prioritize Local Community Benefit & Resilience

The primary purpose of the Temagami Regional Mesh is to serve the people who live, cottage, work, and visit on and around Lake Temagami. We prioritize emergency preparedness, reliable communications during outages or storms, and practical day-to-day uses that make island and remote life safer and more connected.

## 5. Be Genuinely Inclusive & Accessible

We welcome participants of every skill level — from complete beginners who just want a simple node to experienced radio amateurs and tinkerers. No gatekeeping, no condescension. We provide beginner-friendly onboarding, clear documentation, and patient answers.

## 6. Share Knowledge Generously & Document Everything

If you figure something out, write it down and share it. Good documentation, photos of installs, range-test results, configuration examples, and troubleshooting guides multiply the value for everyone. We maintain a public knowledge base so knowledge does not stay siloed in private chats.

## 7. Operate Legally, Responsibly & Considerately

We comply with the relevant Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) regulations. We use appropriate power levels, avoid harmful interference, and remain mindful of other users of the band. When the mesh carries traffic, we respect that emergency or priority messages may need precedence. Good node operators make the whole system better.

## 8. Respect Privacy, Property & the Environment

- Obtain explicit permission before placing any node or antenna on private property, shared docks, or structures.
- Be thoughtful about user privacy and what data is broadcast over the mesh.
- Design deployments that respect the lake’s natural aesthetics and leave no trace.

## 9. Collaborate on Network Health & Thoughtful Growth

Adding nodes or changing settings affects everyone. We coordinate major router placements, channel plans, and firmware versions to keep the mesh reliable, redundant, and performant. We favour quality and coverage over sheer quantity of nodes. Growth is welcomed but should remain manageable.

## 10. Resolve Disagreements Respectfully & Keep It Enjoyable

We assume good intent. When conflicts or differing opinions arise (technical choices, node placement, project direction), we discuss them openly and constructively, focusing on evidence and the project’s long-term health. Personal attacks, harassment, or abusive behaviour have no place here. At the end of the day, this remains a voluntary hobby project.

## How these principles work in practice

- New members are pointed to the Principles early so expectations are clear.
- For core infrastructure (shared routers, primary channels, map curation), the Organization aims for rough consensus among active contributors. Smaller experiments and personal nodes are autonomous.
- Most issues are resolved through respectful discussion in community channels or on GitHub. Persistent or serious problems may be brought to project maintainers and, if needed, to the Board under Article 15.
- The public community edition of the Principles is a living document. A change that would repeal or materially weaken a numbered principle in section 2.03 requires a by-law amendment.

The Temagami Regional Mesh is built on the Meshtastic firmware, apps and ecosystem and on related open-source tools. The Organization shall contribute back where it reasonably can and shall encourage Members to support upstream projects.
