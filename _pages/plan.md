## Feasibility Assessment ##

During the summer of 2026 we designed and deployed six test nodes of various hardware designs to study and narrow down our hardware requirements. We performed range tests and successfully transmitted a LoRa packet 56 km between Maple Mountain and High Rock to demonstrate the potential of the technology. And we installed our first couple of node sites to figure out costs and benefits. The outcome of this feasibility assessment yielded the following key findings:

1. That to accomplish our goals, we will need to deploy approximately 20 solar powered nodes on high structures and hilltops in the region.
2. The cost to deploy a node is $300, requires 30 minutes to assemble and 3 hours to install. It would require approximately 2 hours to remove or repair an already deployed node.
3. That the total cost to implement this regional network is approximately $6000, assuming all volunteer labor and zero government impediments.
4. That it would not be possible to do this project using only private property as nearly all private property is at low altitudes.
5. That there is likely sufficient interest in volunteering and donating to sustain the project.
   
We also developed a standardized node design that:

1. Is easy to deploy and manage
2. Runs on solar power and batteries
3. Is power efficient, has sufficient electrical protections, and has a well designed RF front end
4. That is robust enough to potentially survive being left in harsh remote locations for years of unattended operation

## Demonstration Deployment ##

Our intention now is to deploy 10 identical nodes before the snow flies and observe them over the winter as a temporary deployment that will last no longer than 12 months. The purpose of this temporary deployment is to:

1. Validate the cold weather performance and reliability of our node configuration, and to improve the design.
2. Discover the operational requirements of a long-term and large-scale mesh network deployment.
3. To discover public reactions, complaints, and adverse consequences caused by such a network.
4. To ensure the project can remain viable while hindered by competing land claims, red tape and bureaucracy.
5. To demonstrate the benefits of the technology to community groups, aboriginal groups, and commercial businesses.

At the end of this demonstration deployment, a decision will be made as to whether to continue the project or to permanently remove the nodes from the region.
